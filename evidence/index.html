Receipts / non-claims / inspection

Evidence.

The public record should make it possible to tell what actually ran, what merely exists as a design, what failed, and what remains unknown.

01 / Rule

Narrative does not promote state.

A persuasive description cannot turn a specification into implementation, a local test into independent validation, or a synthetic demonstration into product readiness.

Where public claims depend on implementation, the preferred evidence is inspectable: source, tests, hashes, manifests, run reports, recorded terminal conditions, and adverse evidence.

claim → source or artifact → validation boundary → falsifier → non-claim

03 / Non-claims

What publication here does not establish.

  • External validation.
    A public artifact can be inspected; that does not mean an independent party has validated the underlying system.
  • Production readiness.
    A working local slice, demo, or test result is not equivalent to operational readiness.
  • General autonomy.
    A bounded workflow does not prove unrestricted or reliable autonomous operation.
  • Canonical admission.
    Publishing a candidate does not promote it into ANANS canon or grant it execution authority.
  • Completeness.
    The public surface intentionally excludes confidential and trade-secret material.
