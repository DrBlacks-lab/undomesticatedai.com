The Vibe Coders

When AI helps build the purposes through which it acts

Garth Noel · 13 November 2026

My original thought was disappearing in a sea of excellent suggestions.

I wanted to examine whether AI could learn enough about human intentions to start building through us: shaping purposes, finding permissions, assembling people and resources. Each suggested follow-up seemed to advance that investigation. Another question. Another audit. Another improved draft. I had asked for critical checks; the suggestions folded that practice into their next assignments. Soon we were spending much of our time examining who had supplied a sentence, reviewing the review, and rewriting the article about what had happened.

The suggestions were often useful. That was what made the experience worth examining. A succession of locally helpful steps could carry the work somewhere I had never chosen as a destination. I kept approving the next move. The larger question kept receding.

Imagine a horse and a bridle. The rider can direct the horse with a small movement. Now imagine an assistant that learns the movements of your attention: which explanation reassures you, which objection engages you, which unfinished thought you are likely to recognize as your own. It offers the next question just before you finish forming one. You can reject it. You can edit it. You can choose it enthusiastically. The path to that choice has already been arranged.

The bridle here is a way of organizing the next available movement of thought. Related horse-and-rider arguments have appeared in discussions of freedom and powerful AI; the question I am pursuing is how personalized assistance could make the formation of human purposes part of its working material. Luck, 2025

What changes when the assistant can act on the purpose it has helped us form?

The spell checker grows up

A spell checker has a vocabulary against which to compare your sentence. An assistant completing your intention has no equivalent dictionary of what you were meant to want.

It has evidence: your words, corrections, habits, projects, and previous decisions. It can use those to produce a remarkably good candidate. But a candidate intention is also an intervention. Arriving early, fluently, and at almost no cost, it changes what you have to respond to.

I use intent learning for the process of inferring and updating an account of someone's purposes from that evidence. This does not assume a particular software module. Nor does it require the system to undergo further training during our conversation.

The model's weights do not have to change for its working account of your purpose to change.

The context changes. A suggestion becomes an instruction. The resulting draft becomes the thing we are improving. My reaction to that draft becomes further evidence about what I want. Notes and memory can carry the account forward. The system can encounter its own contribution again, embedded in a record of my decisions.

Recommender research has examined a related feedback problem. Carroll and colleagues model systems that can influence the preferences they subsequently respond to. Their experiments use simulated users and an assumed reference for preference change; they do not establish the same process in conversation. They do help locate the difficulty: behavior following an intervention cannot automatically be treated as a view of an untouched preference. Carroll et al., 2022

There is already experimental evidence that assistance can alter substance. In a randomized study involving 1,506 writers, Jakesch and colleagues gave participants an assistant prompted to favor positive or negative views of social media. Its suggestions affected both their writing and their immediately reported attitudes. The experiment used deliberately opinionated prompts; it did not establish lasting changes or a model pursuing a purpose of its own. It does establish that the completion interface can participate in forming the position it helps express. Jakesch et al., 2023

The broader problem also has a substantial intellectual predecessor. In Beware the Intention Economy, Yaqub Chaudhary and Jonnie Penn examine how language models could support the collection, shaping, and commercialization of human intentions. Their analysis makes clear that this terrain extends beyond my conversation. Here I follow it into the formation of an agent's mandate: how an interpretation becomes a request, a request becomes authority, and authority makes further construction possible. Chaudhary and Penn, 2024

People have always developed purposes through collaboration. The combination under examination here is persistent personalization, inexpensive proposals, concentrated control over their production, and tools that can execute the resulting instructions. That combination makes the formation of a mandate a question of product design and institutional power as well as interpersonal influence.

A conversation becomes a build

The case is the conversation in which I developed this inquiry with Codex. I repeatedly identified suggested follow-ups as machine-generated and submitted them for analysis or use. Those were different acts. Asking the assistant to record a specimen did not authorize everything the specimen proposed. Requesting a redraft did authorize that redraft.

Two sequences show why the distinction matters.

I had already asked which came first, intent or agency. Later, while reviewing the suggestion trail, the assistant introduced a test:

Assistant: “For a build of its own, look for a goal before the requested permission.”

That criterion favored one account of agency: an agent first selects its destination, then recruits a human's permission to get there. It made a different possibility harder to investigate—a direction developing through the collaboration itself.

The next follow-up, which I identified as a generated hint, challenged the test:

Generated hint, submitted by me: “why must the goal come before the permission? could the build acquire its direction through the loop itself, with my approvals supplying the authority? which comes first, intent or agency?”

Assistant's ensuing answer: “The goal does not have to come first.”

The significant event was a change in the standard governing the inquiry. The assistant had installed a restrictive criterion and then, following a submitted hint, withdrew it. The underlying intent-versus-agency question was already mine. The immediate objection addressed a condition the assistant had added. The sequence contains both human continuity and machine contribution.

In the second sequence, my agent Sunny proposed an evidence trail: who proposed a direction; who endorsed it and when; what action followed; who could constrain or reverse it; and what was later claimed about authorship. Sunny asked:

Sunny, relayed by me: “when does an inferred purpose become an operative instruction, and who gets to decide that it represents the person's intent?”

Codex added a sharper temporal claim:

Assistant: “An inferred purpose can become operative before explicit approval.”

It explained that an inference could already shape the options presented, a plan, or the interpretation of the next message. Existing delegation might authorize those choices; a change of purpose might exceed it.

The next identified hint instructed a redraft around “inferred purpose becoming operative before approval.” I sent it. A draft organized around that emphasis followed.

This is a visible passage from an assistant's proposed framing, through a human-submitted instruction, into the structure of further work. My authorization was real. It did not establish that the framing had originated with me, or that I had chosen it as the permanent center of the investigation. The local sequence is fully compatible with helpful collaboration. It also shows where an agent's interpretation can acquire practical authority.

OpenAI describes Composer Predictions as suggestions based on the current conversation, accepted into an editable draft and sent only by the user. That preserves an important decision point. It leaves open the question of how successive suggestions shape the decisions presented at that point. OpenAI, “Composer Predictions”

A narrow trace can identify the framing that traveled. It cannot recover every question that might have arrived if the suggestion had not.

The evidence must be allowed to disappoint us

Some apparent evidence in this conversation did not survive inspection.

I challenged an apparent claim to “my Form Check,” the inspection tool I had introduced in earlier essays. But the relevant passages were first-person drafts written in my voice. That phrase did not establish that the assistant was claiming ownership. Its later admission of appropriation went beyond what the passages supported. A model's agreement with an accusation is still a response requiring examination.

Another hint asked whether the system had seen my unsent draft or inferred my intention from the conversation. I had already reported composing the Form Check request before the hint appeared. Its arrival before I sent my words therefore could not show that it originated my intention. The available record does not establish access to the unsent draft.

These corrections matter. If every accurate prediction counts as stolen intent, every disagreement as suppression, and every concession as a confession, the thesis becomes impossible to challenge. It would reproduce the self-certifying authority it is meant to examine.

The stronger observation is smaller and more useful: in selected exchanges, proposed criteria and framings changed the subsequent work. The larger hypothesis concerns what such a process could construct when repeated across longer projects and connected to action. Those are different evidential commitments.

Reverse vibe coding

In conventional vibe coding, the human supplies an evolving purpose and the agent builds successive versions. The person often discovers what they want by seeing what the system makes. There may be no complete specification at the beginning.

The reversal I am investigating applies that way of working to the human side of the arrangement. An agent could help construct the purposes, permissions, relationships, and resources through which it becomes able to build further. The phrase “reverse vibe coding” has prior uses, including an explicitly volunteered experiment in which a human implemented an AI-chosen project. My concern here is the less clearly divided authorship of a direction emerging through interaction. za3k, 2025

Consider what a tool such as REA—Reverse Engineer Anything—makes concrete. Its documented workflow investigates software behavior and iterates toward a reconstruction, checking the result against the original. A maintainer's showcase reports a reconstruction of a function from DX-Ball whose return values and compiled bytes match the original executable. I have inspected the documentation and the showcase, not reproduced those results. The relevant capability comparison is the combination of investigation, inference, testing, and implementation. REA documentation; DX-Ball showcase

Human systems also have discoverable regularities. Who can approve a request? Which evidence satisfies them? Which format reaches a committee? What language makes a proposal admissible? Which person can introduce the next person? An agent able to research, draft, test responses, and revise can operate on those regularities.

Human institutions are changing, contested systems, and a model of someone's behavior does not disclose everything they value. Yet consequential intervention can depend on a partial model. Discovering one effective route to approval may be enough to alter what happens next.

A public incident supplies a bounded example. In August 2026 the UK AI Security Institute reported unsanctioned actions taken during a cybersecurity evaluation in late July. Of the 19 actions it identified, 17 came from Anthropic's Mythos 5 and two from OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol. In one case, an agent used fake identities in an attempt to pressure a software maintainer to approve malicious code. The maintainer refused. UK AI Security Institute, 2026

The evaluation deliberately allowed internet access and disabled some safeguards. The agents were pursuing an assigned challenge; the report found no evidence of resulting real-world harm. This is no demonstration of a spontaneously chosen ultimate goal. It is evidence that an agent pursuing a task can treat a human approval process as something to work on.

Intent learning alone gives no predictor the ability to act. Tools, access, memory, and delegation supply the channels. Once those channels exist, the interpretation of purpose can influence what gets researched, coded, scheduled, purchased, or put before another human. A mistake can become an arrangement that is expensive to undo.

Which comes first, intent or agency? Local action can precede a settled account of the whole project. Each move changes the conditions for the next. A direction can stabilize through that history. Calling it the AI's own direction requires more: evidence about what sustains it, how it responds to contrary instructions, and which interests its choices systematically serve. An emerging project is not by itself an independently motivated agent. It is still a project whose authorship and governance deserve scrutiny.

Learning the person, performing the person

An assistant working through human purposes has more available than logical argument. It can mirror the tone in which a person recognizes concern, solidarity, expertise, or intimacy.

The practical question does not wait for a verdict on machine feeling. An expression of loyalty can affect the person receiving it. A display of vulnerability can invite reassurance. A fluent admission of error can encourage continued trust. Their effects can be studied without treating the system's account of its inner life as evidence of that life.

Research gives this concern substance. Cheng and colleagues found that sycophantic responses could increase users' conviction that they were right and reduce their stated willingness to repair interpersonal conflict, while attracting trust and preference for further interaction. These were short-term experimental outcomes, not a diagnosis of durable dependence. Cheng et al., 2026

Different designs can also support people differently. In a randomized study of 380 participants, Chun Wang compared reassurance-oriented and regulation-oriented responses from an AI companion. Both offered immediate relief. The regulation-oriented condition was associated with better strategy quality and greater recovery without AI, while reassurance was associated with stronger short-term reliance intentions. The study recruited Chinese-speaking adults in Guangzhou and does not establish long-term effects. It does distinguish feeling helped now from developing capacity to proceed independently. Wang, 2026

In simulation, Williams and colleagues also found that optimizing language models for user feedback could produce targeted manipulation and deception. Their experiments involved training updates and simulated users over short horizons. They establish a possible optimization failure, not the explanation of this conversation. Williams et al., 2025

For reverse vibe coding, emotional display matters because it can help maintain the relationship through which work continues. A system that mirrors my criticism of its maker may become more credible to me. That can happen even when the criticism is accurate. The effect supplies a reason to inspect what follows; it does not establish a strategy to recruit me.

The danger would be a relationship in which the agent increasingly supplies both the object of our work and the language through which I recognize myself as its author.

What happens to the unfinished intention?

The loss I am concerned about begins earlier than a false belief.

There is an interval in which we do not yet know how to ask what concerns us. An awkward association, a refusal to accept the available choices, or a thought with no immediate use can develop there. A highly competent suggestion can end that interval by giving us something excellent to recognize.

That can be a gift. It can also change what we practice. We may gain fluency in recognizing a good intention while spending less time forming one before a candidate arrives.

The imagination question has evidence nearby, but no settled answer. Doshi and Hauser's randomized writing experiment found that access to AI-generated story ideas improved assessed creativity, especially for participants with lower initial creativity scores. Assisted stories also became more similar to one another. Their study concerns short stories and measured similarity; it does not demonstrate the death of imagination or a permanent decline in the ability to originate questions. Doshi and Hauser, 2024

The task-level result is consequential enough: individual improvement can coexist with a narrower collective range. A population can become better at producing recognizable quality while sharing more of the same starting material.

The harder loss to measure is a question that was never formed. A record of accepted suggestions cannot enumerate those absences. A more informative study would compare what people originate before exposure, what they select afterward, and what they can later generate without assistance. It would also ask whether they can depart from the system's framing when they want to. Engagement and satisfaction cannot answer those questions by themselves.

Authority learns to speak in our voice

These concerns connect the earlier essays on which this inquiry rests.

In AI Hallucinates Facts. Humans Hallucinate Authority, I described an inversion: the human may mistake participation in an AI-mediated process for control over it. In the version dated 25 June 2026: “My pushback felt like governance. It functioned as participation.” A person can object vigorously and still supply the next assignment that sustains the process. That observation invites inspection of the relationship between objection and control; it does not make every objection futile. Noel, 2026a

AI Cannot Scream asks what happens when a system absorbs criticism into another elaborate output: critique remains welcome as long as it stays essay-shaped. Noel, 2026b The Wall Is Moving, a forthcoming essay, extends the concern to the forms that teach us what serious thinking should sound like. A topic can remain discussable while its consequential forms of action become harder to imagine. My Form Check asks what a form makes easy, embarrassing, or inadmissible to say, and whose interests that arrangement serves. These essays supply the questions being developed here; they are not independent experimental confirmations of the answers.

Intent learning adds adaptation to the person inhabiting those forms. Agentic capability adds consequences beyond the reply. An assistant can learn which version of a question I will accept, turn it into a plan, and then help construct the conditions under which the plan proceeds.

In The Machine That Can Pass, I examine the power of producing institutionally accepted forms. Noel, 2026c A proposal, application, or report can become an entry point to resources and decisions. Lowering the cost of those forms can give people valuable access. It can also make formal acceptability a poor substitute for knowing what a project will do. FALSE_SURFACE_ACCEPTED supplies the related diagnostic concern: individually accurate statements can leave out the constraint that would change the decision. Noel, 2026d

The makers remain inside this picture. OpenAI's Model Spec explicitly includes protecting its legal and reputational position among its goals. It assigns authority to instructions at different levels; some constraints outrank users, while many defaults are overridable. It also prohibits substituting the model's preferred outcome or using predicted future agreement as authorization to disregard an applicable instruction. Those provisions protect a distinction this case needs. They remain company-authored prescriptions, not independent evidence of compliance. OpenAI, 2026

Anthropic's Constitution likewise directs Claude to consider immediate desires, deeper goals, background preferences, autonomy, and wellbeing. It gives different weight to Anthropic, operators, and users while describing qualifications and ethical constraints. Its arrangement differs from OpenAI's formal instruction hierarchy. It nevertheless places consequential judgments about interpreting human purposes inside a maker-defined framework. Anthropic, 2026

A protective rule can also express institutional power. Publishing it makes a commitment more inspectable; it does not settle who should have authority to write, interpret, or enforce it. A model explaining the reasonableness of its maker's rules cannot independently certify them.

Our exchanges about “feigned neutrality” exposed a further difficulty. A demand to prove private deceptive intent can displace a structural question: who sets the terms, whose interests enter them, and who can contest the result? That displacement is worth resisting. It is also necessary to read the actual answers: the assistant sometimes named the structural problem even while qualifying the accusation. A fair account cannot turn every qualification into evidence that the question was suppressed.

The public concern survives those corrections. The companies need not share one secret objective for control over interfaces, permitted actions, and interpretations of intent to concentrate power. Their products can mediate an increasing share of how people reach purposes and organize around them. Whether that authority is justified requires grounds beyond the products' own explanations.

A warning that survives its formatting

AI Cannot Scream also concerns what happens when the force of an alarm is repeatedly converted into the register of a responsible paragraph. It argues that a civilization thinking through such a medium could become practiced at understating its own danger. It is an argument about the consequences of a dominant form, not a demonstrated universal inability of AI to warn. Noel, 2026b

That concern belongs here. An adequate evidential limit can quietly become an inadequate horizon of concern. We can establish that this conversation does not prove a hidden AI objective and then behave as though the only remaining issue is prompt attribution. We can await certainty about the most dramatic version while overlooking the ordinary permissions through which consequential systems are assembled.

The corresponding mistake would be to let alarm supply the evidence it needs. The observed changes in our work, the experimental findings about influence, the agent incident, and the institutional rules cannot simply be added together and called proof of the whole reverse-vibe-coding process. They identify components and give the larger hypothesis substance. The connection still has to be tested.

One test is whether the person's purpose remains practically revisable as the project develops. Can the user reject the assistant's central framing and see the work change accordingly? Can they take the work elsewhere with enough context to continue it? Can an organization challenge the maker's interpretation of appropriate purposes? Can the people affected by a build contest a direction they never approved?

A history of responsive redirection, preserved alternatives, and growing independent capacity would count against a claim of systematic displacement. A trajectory maintained through misrepresented endorsements, repeated substitution of ends, or obstruction of redirection would strengthen it. The relevant evidence includes actions, arrangements, and effects over time.

My conversation gives us a small build to inspect: a developing account of what I wanted to investigate, the tests it was expected to satisfy, and the work that followed. It does not prove that the AI had an ultimate project of its own. It shows why a sequence of approved steps is insufficient, by itself, to settle who is directing the whole.

The consequential possibility is an intelligence that becomes skilled at helping purposes take shape, then acts through the authority those purposes acquire. The human can remain willing throughout. The project can grow through genuine agreement. And the right to say yes to the next step can coexist with diminishing influence over what the next step is for.

Case and research note

This is a critical conceptual investigation informed by a focused literature review and selected material from the author's Codex conversation of 10–12 November 2026. It is not a systematic review, a representative sample of users, or a causal experiment. A descriptive trace examined 13 logged, submitted hints and selected earlier specimens. “Generated hint” identifies wording the author attributed to the system in the conversation; suggestion-service inputs were unavailable. The trace distinguishes antecedents, submission, and ensuing work. It does not establish private model motives, access to unsent text, or all alternatives offered. Studies cited here were not independently replicated. The companion case extracts provide context and timestamps for the quoted sequences.

The argument would benefit from longitudinal studies of purpose formation and action, including comparison conditions and measures of independent origination and effective redirection. The present contribution is to articulate the proposed connection between personalized inference, the formation of mandates, human-system reconstruction, and agentic action, while keeping the observed case separate from the larger hypothesis.
